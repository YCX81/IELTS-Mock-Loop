# Column Style Reference

Read this when the user asks for Chinese foreign-media intensive reading, says “按原型/栏目格式”, or when output quality depends on natural column-style explanation rather than a worksheet.

## Core Rhythm

- Start with a natural Chinese title and 1-3 short intro paragraphs.
- The intro should give the reader an entry point: what the article seems to discuss, what tension it really contains, and why the language matters.
- Do not expose internal planning fields such as “一句话主旨”, “主线抓手”, “讲解密度分层”, “先抓三个核心表达”, or “本文重点看 A/B/C”.
- Proceed with numbered reading units: number, English sentence or sentence group, natural Chinese translation, then explanatory Chinese paragraphs.
- Avoid repeated labels such as “自然译文”, “段落作用”, “表达提示”, “为什么好”, “迁移使用”. Use natural transitions instead.

## What A Good Explanation Does

- Explain the sentence in context before explaining the word.
- For a high-value expression, cover: meaning in this sentence, word image or construction, difference from ordinary wording, tone and usage boundary, function in the article, migration examples, and nearby alternatives.
- External material should behave like an example bank for the expression, not a source packet for the article topic. Use it the way a column explains `backfire`, `tainted`, or `be styled like`: real context first, then reusable bilingual sentences and nearby expressions.
- Use “这句话的关键在……”, “这里不是简单说……，而是在写……”, “这个词比普通的……更有……语气”, and “以后写……话题时，可以借……” as natural moves, not as visible headings.
- Keep one main point per ordinary unit. A title, dense sentence, or core metaphor may carry 2-4 learning points, but still needs hierarchy.
- Standard mode should usually keep only 3-4 A-layer expressions. For short texts or excerpts under about 800 English words, use at most 5 unless the user explicitly asks for deep expansion.

## Genre Direction

- Social/news controversy: trace intention, wording, public reaction, institutional response, and the article’s distance from each party.
- Business, consumer-brand, and cross-cultural writing: explain “market move + cultural translation + authorial tone”. Show what market the brand is entering, how a habit is repackaged, and how titles use puns, brand suffixes, or light irony.
- Technical or idea essays: identify the load-bearing concept, the author’s judgment, and expressions that compress abstract mechanisms.
- Digital/platform-economy writing: distinguish user convenience, worker cost, incentives, and institutional blind spots.
- Literature or narrative nonfiction: keep plot clear, then explain imagery, voice, pacing, and character judgment.

## Useful Treatment Patterns

- Titles and subtitles: stop for puns, metaphors, brand suffixes, compressed contrast, and tone. For example, `a competition is brewing` should connect both drink-making and conflict slowly forming.
- Market expressions: `gain a foothold` means first finding a stable place to stand, not immediately winning; `Starbucks-style` means borrowing a playbook or cultural model, not merely looking like Starbucks.
- Numbers: explain the proportion and judgment the numbers create. Do not repeat every number at equal weight.
- Interview quotes: explain what abstract claim the quote makes concrete, such as consumer psychology, identity, price perception, or street-level scene.
- Sensitive or slang origins: explain the boundary clearly. Knowing a word’s history does not mean learners should actively use it.

## Ending

Close by returning to the title, the article’s main tension, or the 3-5 things the reader should truly take away. Do not append exercises, tables, or cards unless requested.
